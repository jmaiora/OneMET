About the app

OneMET helps you see how physical activity affects your glucose and plan carbohydrates around your workouts. It reads glucose from Nightscout, Dexcom Share or LibreLinkUp (or Apple Health), and workouts, activity and METs from Apple Health and your Apple Watch.

  • Summary — your glucose today, with the before/during/after curve of any workout, what to do before exercising, activity rings and your MET·min over the last week.
  • Plan — pick a sport, duration and intensity, and get a prevention-first fuel plan: whether to start now, a timeline of carbohydrate intakes during the session, what to carry, and what to do afterwards.
  • Workouts — your session history, each with its own glucose curve and an insight on how it affected you.
  • Settings — your profile, targets, glucose source, and Help & FAQ.

Available in English and Spanish, in mg/dL or mmol/L.

Frequently asked

Which iPhone do I need?

An iPhone running iOS 16 or later. An Apple Watch and a CGM (via Nightscout, Dexcom Share or LibreLinkUp) make the most of the app, but aren't strictly required to try it.

How do I connect my glucose data?

Choose a source during the first-run setup, or later in Settings → Glucose Source: enter your Nightscout site, your Dexcom Share account or your LibreLinkUp follower account. Connections are read-only, and passwords are kept in the iPhone Keychain. Dexcom Share keeps about 24 h of data and LibreLinkUp about 12 h, so 14-day figures come from Nightscout or Apple Health. Medtronic CareLink isn't supported.

Who is the fuel plan for?

The carbohydrate guidance is designed for people using insulin — type 1 diabetes, and type 2 on insulin, by pump or injections. For anyone else the Plan tab explains why instead of giving a number; the rest of the app works the same.

How does the plan work out the carbohydrates?

Before: whether to start, eat first or wait comes from your current glucose and trend arrow, and from whether the exercise usually lowers glucose (cardio) or keeps it steady or raises it (strength, intervals). During: intakes are planned at about 0.3–0.5 g per kg per hour when only basal insulin is active, and 0.5–1.0 g with active bolus insulin, never more than 60 g an hour; starting above your exercise target counts as fuel. The thresholds adapt to how regularly you exercise and how prone you are to lows. After: what to take in the following 90 minutes and before bed. Settings → Help & FAQ explains each step.

What about strength training and HIIT?

They lower glucose much less than steady cardio and can even raise it, so the plan doesn't schedule carbohydrates during them: you take some only if you drop. It also warns that glucose may rise afterwards and that a delayed low can come overnight.

I use a closed-loop pump. Can I use the plan?

The app assumes you're in control of your insulin. A closed loop's sport mode raises its target but doesn't know how hard you're exercising, and the system keeps adjusting insulin by itself, so the carbohydrates you'll need can't be planned in advance — and the exercise guidelines the plan is built on state that their tables don't apply to closed loops. On a closed loop, switch to manual mode before and during the activity, and use the plan as you would on an open loop. Agree this with your clinician first.

Can I change how often I take carbs during a session?

Yes. The plan spaces intakes every 45 minutes by default; on the fuel plan you can choose 30, 45 or 60 minutes, and the most you'd take at once (20, 30 or 45 g) — whichever you feel most comfortable with. The session total stays the same; a shorter interval just means smaller, more frequent intakes. The default interval is in Settings → Profile → Carb Intake Interval.

My Apple Watch workouts don't appear.

Check that OneMET still has access in Settings → Privacy & Security → Health → OneMET, with Workouts, Heart Rate and Active Energy turned on. iOS updates occasionally reset these permissions; if the switches are missing entirely, deleting and reinstalling OneMET and granting access again fixes it.

Is my data private?

Your health data stays on your device and is used only to show your own trends and guidance. Glucose is fetched directly from the service you connect; there is no OneMET server, and nothing is sold or shared with third parties.

I'm a beta tester and can't install the app.

Make sure the free TestFlight app is installed, and that you opened the invitation on the same iPhone using the email linked to your Apple ID. If it still fails, email me the address you used.

OneMET provides illustrative, educational guidance only. It is not a medical device and does not replace advice from your healthcare team. Always agree insulin and carbohydrate decisions with your clinician.

The fuel plan draws on the EASD/ISPAD 2020 position statement on glucose management for exercise with CGM (Moser, Riddell et al., Diabetologia 2020) and the ISPAD 2022 exercise guidelines (Adolfsson et al., Pediatric Diabetes 2022). Both are expert consensus, the individual thresholds are published, and the way this app combines them has not been validated as an algorithm. Their tables were not written for hybrid closed-loop systems in automatic mode.
